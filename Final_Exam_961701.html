The case: Ping River Health Network

The Ping River Health Network links five community hospitals in Chiang Mai and Lamphun (H1–H5) and their sub-district health promotion hospitals. They share one registry of about 9,000 adults with type 2 diabetes. Every registered patient is invited to an annual diabetes review: weight and blood pressure, a foot examination, HbA1c, creatinine (eGFR) and urine albumin, and a medication review. Lab results come back two days after the visit.

Every year about one reviewed patient in six has a serious complication within the following 12 months: a hospital admission for hyperglycaemic crisis or severe hypoglycaemia, a diabetic foot ulcer or infection needing admission, an amputation, a fall in eGFR below 45 (CKD stage 3b or worse), or sight-threatening retinopathy. Each complication costs the network on average 70,000 THB and often costs the patient their independence.

Dr. Anong (Medical Director): “At every annual review I want to know which patients are heading for a complication in the next year, so we can do something about it — and I want proof that what we do actually works.”

Khun Pim (NCD Nurse Manager) runs a nurse case-management programme: monthly phone follow-up, home foot care, medication adherence and a quick route to the doctor. It costs about 3,000 THB per patient per year, and places are limited. In 2024 she ran a randomised pilot of the programme.

Khun Wit (Network CFO) has to reserve places in the provincial hospital’s diabetic-foot surgery list a year in advance and wants the model to tell him how many.

Khun Mai (IT Manager): a vendor model (v0) went live on 1 October 2026. It scores each patient at their annual review. The data warehouse refreshes nightly at 02:00. Admissions to hospitals outside the network arrive through national insurance claims up to 90 days late.

The Data Protection Officer: health data are sensitive personal data under the PDPA. Some patients have withdrawn consent for their data to be used for analytics.

B210 pointsChoosing how to split the data.

Scenario. The network wants to build the complication model. The data contains 18,000 annual reviews from 6,200 patients at hospitals H1–H5, with index visits from January 2021 to June 2025 (most patients have one review per year). The outcome needs 12 months of follow-up plus up to 90 days for claims from outside hospitals. The model will be used from next year on all current patients at their next review, and also at a sixth hospital in Chiang Rai that joins the network in 2027.

The analyst proposes: “Shuffle all 18,000 reviews, run 5-fold cross-validation, try 60 hyper-parameter settings, keep the setting with the best mean ROC-AUC, and report that mean ROC-AUC (0.81) to Dr. Anong.”
